  [673] Making food is one thing; keeping it edible is another. In their natural state, all the organic materials we eat are perishable. Even before they become completely unappetising, foods lose nutritional value as vitamins break down due to heat, strong light, air, or water. In preserving food, the first priority is to keep yeasts, moulds, bacteria, and insects from consuming it before we do. Certain bacteria can overwhelm a person's digestive tract or release dangerous toxins, causing the misery of acute food poisoning. People have tried to prevent food spoilage since they first began cooking meat over a fire — smoking, salting, drying, and pickling have existed for thousands of years. The science of food preservation, however, advanced considerably in the 19th century with the introduction of processes such as pasteurisation.
